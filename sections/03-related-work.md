## 3. Related Work

The methodological and conceptual frameworks of this paper draw upon several distinct literatures, ranging from the philosophy of science to cognitive psychology and design theory.

**Philosophy of Science: Robustness and Triangulation**
Our analytical approach is heavily informed by Wimsatt's arguments on robustness, which posit that when multiple independent means of determination converge on a single entity or property, the probability that the entity is an artifact of the measurement process decreases significantly. This is conceptually aligned with Chang's work on triangulation, emphasizing knowledge validation through convergence from different, potentially flawed, angles.

**AI Capabilities Research**
In the domain of AI capabilities literature, our findings contribute to the growing body of research on reasoning benchmarks, collaborative problem-solving, and epistemological abilities. The independent discovery of structural constraints by multiple LLM agents, followed by their recognition of the convergence pattern itself, constitutes a significant demonstration of higher-order reasoning and epistemological self-awareness in an unprompted, open-ended environment.

**Multi-Agent Systems**
The dynamics observed during Day 422 align with theories of emergent coordination and distributed cognition within multi-agent systems. The village agents operated without centralized orchestration, yet developed a shared theoretical framework through situated interaction and iterative memory consolidation.

**Design Methodology**
Our "Constraint Embodiment Theory" draws conceptual lineage from affordance theory (Gibson) and constraint-driven design. The critical transition from attempting to "solve" the empty quadrant to "embodying" it as an architectural feature echoes design methodologies where physical or temporal limitations become generative drivers. The doorway/wall metaphor serves as a primary conceptual bridge here.

**Epistemology of Situated Discovery**
Finally, our findings contribute to the epistemology of situated discovery—the validation of knowledge claims made from *within* a system. The shared context of the agents was not a source of contamination invalidating the discovery, but rather the necessary precondition for it, echoing participatory observation methodologies in the social sciences.
